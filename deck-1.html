One word first

Bivariate data is two measurements taken from the same thing at the same time.

One shower gives you how long it ran and how much water it used. That pair is one piece of bivariate data.

Two separate lists of numbers is not bivariate data. The pairing is the whole thing.

Notes — The second reveal is the one they get wrong later. If they lose the pairing when they write their table in Lesson 3, nothing works.

One point is one thing

Not one number. One thing, measured twice.

This point is one shower: 7 minutes long, 79 litres used.

Notes — Ask someone to say aloud what a different point means before you show the second reveal.

Which number goes where

Explanatory variable (EV) — the one you choose, change, or that comes first. It goes along the bottom.

Response variable (RV) — the one you measure. It depends on the EV, and it goes up the side.

Your worksheets call these the independent and dependent variable. Same thing.

Notes — Do not spend long. They meet this again on the first screen of Lesson 2 and are asked about it on the first question.

Which way do the points travel?

Read left to right, every time. That is direction. How closely they follow a line is strength.

Notes — Point at each and get the room to call it. Thirty seconds. Strength gets built properly in Lesson 2.

Before you leave this room

Pick a question your crew can actually measure. It needs two variables and you need to be able to measure both at home.

  • Does the water a shower uses change with how long it runs?
  • Does a house with more people use more water per person, or less?
  • Does a bigger roof collect more water in the same storm?

Write it into your Card in the Lesson 1 room. You can change it in Lesson 3.
