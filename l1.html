Getting started

What you are doing today

Nothing in this room is marked and there is no code to find. It is a look around before you start. By the end you will have picked the question your crew is going to answer, and written it on your Card.

Two kinds of data

Categorical data is a name or a group. Which suburb you live in. What colour your bottle is. You can count how many fall in each group, but you cannot add them up or find their average.

Numerical data is a number that means something as a number. You can add it, average it and put it on a scale.

Numerical data comes in two sorts, and the difference decides your chart.

  • Discrete — counted, in whole steps. The number of taps in a room. There is no such thing as 4.5 taps.
  • Continuous — measured, and it can land anywhere in between. The length of a shower. It can be 6 minutes, or 6.4, or 6.37.

Your question

Pick something your crew can actually measure, about water, with two variables in it. It does not have to be right yet — you can change it in Lesson 3 when you plan how to collect it.

A question that works has this shape: does A change when B changes? — where you can measure both A and B with what you have at home.

  • Does the water a shower uses change with how long it runs?
  • Does a house with more people use more water per person, or less?
  • Does a bigger roof collect more water in the same storm?
