Four words you need

A sample is the set of measurements you actually take. Your sample size is how many measurements are in it.

A method is the exact steps you follow to take one measurement. A method is repeatable when someone else can read it, follow it, and get the same kind of number you did.

A measurement is one number written in one of your measurement columns. A blank cell is a measurement nobody took.

A crew who got it wrong

This crew wanted to know whether longer showers use more water. Here is what they did.

  • Priya timed her showers on her phone. Marta counted to sixty, four times.
  • Priya caught the water in a 10 litre bucket. Marta read the meter in the front garden, which counts every tap in the house.
  • They took six measurements on Monday and none after that.

Their numbers cannot be compared with each other. Counting to sixty is not the same as a phone, and a meter that counts the whole house is not measuring one shower. Six measurements from one day describe Monday, not showers.

Three things went wrong. The method changed between the two of them. What they measured with changed. And their sample was small and all taken at once.

Now plan your own

Write your plan on your crew's Data Evidence Card: the question you are asking, your two variables with their units, your method, your sample size, and where the data came from.

Then build your collection table in the collection table tool and print the recording sheet to take home. Your badge needs at least ten rows of data in that table, as well as the three questions above.

Write the method so that someone in another crew could follow it without asking you a single question. That is the test.
