Five displays, and what each one is for

A dot plot puts one dot above the number line for each measurement. Measurements with the same value stack up on top of each other.

A histogram sorts the measurements into equal intervals and draws one column for each interval, as tall as the number of measurements in it.

A box plot draws the middle half of the measurements as a box, with a line inside it at the middle value and a line out to each end.

A pie chart cuts a circle into slices, one slice for each category. It shows what share each category has of the whole.

A column graph draws one column for each named thing, as tall as the value of that thing.

The scatterplot you read in Lesson 2 is the sixth. It is the only one of the six that shows two measurements at once.

Scale

The scale is what the numbers on an axis do: where the axis starts, and how big each step between them is. Charts A, B and C below show the same six measurements. Only the scale is different.

Look at each vertical axis on its own before you compare the columns.

Your three questions

Now make your own two charts

Your badge needs two charts on your crew's Card, as well as the three questions above.

  • Chart 1 shows one of your variables on its own. Use the one-variable tool and choose the display that suits your data.
  • Chart 2 is your scatterplot: both variables together, one point for each pair of measurements. Use the two-variable tool.

Both charts need a label and a unit on each axis, and a vertical axis that starts at zero unless you can say why it should not.
