The same numbers, twice

Both charts below show the same six readings. You cannot change the readings. You can only change the axis on the right one. See how far apart you can make the two stories look.

Your axis

What you just did

Every chart you made was true. Not one number changed. That is the point: a chart can be completely accurate and still tell you the wrong thing, and the place to look is always the axis.

Three questions to ask of any chart someone shows you:

  • Where does the vertical axis start? If it is not zero, the differences are being made to look bigger than they are.
  • Are the gaps between the numbers all the same size? If not, part of the chart has been stretched.
  • How much of the data is shown? Cutting off the years that disagree with you is the oldest trick there is.

Now go and find one

Find a real chart about water, or weather, or money, in a newspaper or on a website. Work out which of the three tricks it uses, if any. Plenty of real charts are honest — saying so about an honest one is just as good an answer.
